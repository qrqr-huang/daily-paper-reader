# Fixed-structure Gaussian Mixture Filtering with Robust Measurement Updates under Outliers

Abstract —Bayesian state estimation for discrete-time nonlinear stochastic systems is considered in the presence of measurement outliers. Building on a fixed-structure Gaussian mixture filtering framework, this paper proposes a robust measurement-update variant in which the predictive density structure is determined by an offline decomposition of the transition density into axis-aligned Gaussian components. This construction maintains the Gaussian mixture structure as deterministic and tunable via the chosen decomposition fidelity. Measurement components affected by outliers are modeled using a Student’s-t distribution, and the corresponding update of each Gaussian mixture component is approximated by a variational Bayes procedure. The resulting filter is evaluated in a two-dimensional tracking scenario with a three-dimensional augmented state and range-and-bearing measurements, where the bearing channel is affected by outliers modeled as heavy-tailed noise. 

Index Terms —State estimation; Nonlinear systems; Outliers; Gaussian mixture 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

State estimation is crucial for fields such as tracking, guidance, positioning, navigation, sensor fusion, control, fault detection, and decision-making. Its goal is to estimate the state of a dynamic system from noisy measurements. The Bayesian approach to state estimation involves calculating the posterior probability density function (PDF) of the state con-ditioned on available measurements. For discrete-time prob-lems, it involves alternating between the Bayes equation and Chapman-Kolmogorov equation (CKE), collectively referred to as Bayesian recursive relations (BRRs). BRRs are analyt-ically tractable only for a few special cases, such as linear Gaussian systems, and usually an approximation is required. The approximate solutions to the BRRs yield algorithms of varying complexity. Assuming the joint state and measurement prediction PDF being Gaussian leads to Gaussian-assumed filters (GAFs) (e.g., cubature filter [1] or the stochastic inte-gration filter [2]), which are computationally light. However, this assumption rarely holds, leading to poor Gaussian filter (GF) performance in strongly nonlinear systems. Discrete approximation of the state posterior PDF results in another group of approximate Bayesian algorithms. These include the point-mass filters (PMFs) [3] replacing the con-tinuous support of the posterior by a usually orthogonal and

equidistant grid of weighted points. While such a grid is flexible in representing the posterior, it leads to computation-ally intensive algorithms. Switching from a deterministically constructed grid to randomly positioned points leads to particle filters (PFs) [4]. They are computationally lighter than the PMF. Still, the estimates are influenced by random effects, which can pose significant challenges in safety-critical appli-cations or even render them inadmissible in certified solutions. The gap between GAFs and PMFs is filled by Gaussian mixture filters (GMFs) [5], [6]. They represent the posterior PDF as a mixture of Gaussian PDFs with continuous support. The support, along with smaller variances of each Gaussian mixture (GM) term compared to a single Gaussian PDF, lead to higher accuracy, even in highly nonlinear problems. Compared to PFs and PMFs, the GMFs are generally less computationally intensive. Many GMFs have been proposed, varying in the way how individual components in the GM are handled. Local 

GMFs [7] process each Gaussian component with its own filter and control the inevitable growth of components by pruning or merging, an approach later refined by [8] using a bank of GAFs with rules for updating component weights after correction. More recent work [9] further refines post-correction weight updates using various linearization strate-gies, while [10] employ more sophisticated component-wise filtering through a bank of Gaussian particle filters. 

Global GMFs process all mixture components jointly, be-ginning with [11] posterior parameters are computed via ordi-nary differential equationss (ODEs) instead of approximating the prior mixture, and more recently, including [12], where a deterministic-sample update by weighting prior samples is performed, extracting higher-order moments, and deriving a posterior GM that minimizes Fisher information. Another line of global methods uses GM approximations of the transition density (e.g., [13]), which generally causes exponential growth of components and hence requires complex mixture reduction, whereas in [14] this was avoided by decomposing the transi-tion density into axis-aligned components, enabling a fixed number of components and closed-form posterior expressions. Recently, [15] proposed an efficient fixed-structure GMFs that avoid local component processing and prevent component explosion by decomposing the transition density offline into axis-aligned Gaussian components. Two decompositions were arXiv:2608.19895v2 [eess.SY] 21 Aug 2026 introduced: the decomposition of the filtered state grid and of the predicted state grid , each offering different trade-offs among offline complexity, the need for an approximation domain, and the use of numerical integration. Both decom-positions maintain a fixed number of posterior components, enabling a tunable balance between online computational load and estimation accuracy. Measurement noise often contains outliers caused by un-modeled anomalies or sensor faults, making Gaussian noise assumptions unreliable and motivating the use of heavy-tailed models, such as the Student’s-t distribution. Existing outlier-robust state estimation methods include PDF-based approaches like Gaussian sum, PMFs, and PFs; robust GAFs that overbound noise covariances (often degrading accuracy); and Student’s-t–based filters [16] that offer solid theoretical grounding with low computational cost. Recent advances in the latter group include variational methods [17], Student’s-t process regression [18], and solutions to BRRs for linear and nonlinear systems with heavy-tailed or skewed Student’s-t noise [19], [20]. This paper builds upon the fixed-structure GMF with predicted-state grid decomposition, which offers easier utiliza-tion in higher-dimensional state spaces and greater flexibility in the denseness of the Gaussian terms and state-space cover-age compared to the filtered-state grid. Here, denseness refers to the spatial concentration of mixture terms in the approxi-mation region, not to a probability density function. A robust 

fixed-structure GMF is proposed to address the Bayesian state estimation problem involving outliers in measurements. The novelty lies in the combination of the predicted state grid fixed structure with robust heavy-tailed update. The paper is structured as follows: Section II specifies the estimation problem to be addressed. The BRRs and the fixed-structure GMF are given in Section III. The robust measurement update of the filter addressing the outliers is presented in Section IV. Performance of the proposed filter is demonstrated using a 3D tracking example in Section V, and concluding remarks are drawn in Section VI. II. P ROBLEM STATEMENT

The paper addressed Bayesian state estimation for nonlinear stochastic systems with measurement channels influenced by outliers. A robust measurement-update variant of the fixed-structure GMF was proposed. The fixed structure avoids random sampling effects and uncontrolled mixture growth; its computational benefit depends on the selected decomposition rank and pruning level. Robustness to outliers is achieved by modeling measurement noise with a Student’s-t distribution and employing the variational Bayes method during the mea-surement update of individual Gaussian mixture terms. Numer-ical results demonstrated that the proposed filter consistently outperforms both the Gaussian-assumed unscented Kalman filter and the bootstrap particle filter.

Abstract—Sensors deployed in real-world conditions often produce measurements corrupted by outliers due to model uncertainties, changes in the surrounding environment, and/or data loss. As a result, managing these outliers becomes crucial for state estimation to avoid inaccurate estimations and a reduction in the reliability of results. To address this issue, we introduce a novel form of extended Kalman filter (EKF) based on the maximum a posteriori (MAP) principle for scenarios where outliers simultaneously occur in multiple dimensions. For detecting outliers during the filtering process, we introduce a novel variant of the normalized innovation ratio (NIR) test and embed it within the EKF framework. Our approach enhances the estimation accuracy and computational efficiency of state estimation process even when data from several sensors simultaneously contain outliers. 

Index Terms—Sensor signal processing, extended Kalman filter (EKF), maximum a posteriori (MAP), normalized innovation ratio (NIR), outliers, reduced sequential maximum a posteriori-based (RSMAP), sensors signal processing, state estimation. 

I. INTRODUCTION 

State estimation is a critical problem in engineering and statistics, fundamental to the operation and control of systems in diverse domains, such as power systems [1], robotics [2], and target tracking [3], [4]. At the heart of this problem lies the challenge of accurately predicting system states, a task for which the Kalman filter (KF) and its nonlinear version, the extended Kalman filter (EKF) [5], [6], have been widely adopted. These filters operate under the assumption that noise and system dynamics can be modeled as Gaussian processes, making them exceptionally effective under linear conditions or when nonlinearities can be adequately linearized. However, real-world use of the KF family faces challenges from outliers, i.e., measurements that are significantly different from expected distribution due to sensor issues, environmental disruptions, malicious activities, or data errors [7]–[9]. These anomalies can drastically affect state estimation algorithms, resulting in inaccurate predictions. Outliers may distort data from a single sensor or simultaneously across multiple sensors. Therefore, the strategies for outlier mitigation fall into two categories as well. Some of the initial works, which aim toward managing outliers occurring in a single dimension, are based on robust M-Estimation methods. These methods were developed to mitigate outlier effects by adjusting their influence during the estimation process [10], [11]. Concurrently, 𝑙 1 norm filters approach outlier handling through optimization strategies [12], although their performance can be limited in dynamic environments. Another family of methods, 𝐻 ∞ filtering, aims at ensuring robustness against extreme scenarios [13], yet it may introduce a conservative bias. The main limitation of these methods is their mitigation of outliers in only one dimension. Progressing from these foundational approaches, recent research works have explored non-Gaussian models to represent outliers. These methods mainly deal with outliers occurring in multiple dimensions but had other major drawbacks. As an example, some of the works use the Monte Carlo method [14] and Student-t distributions [15], which require precise parameter tuning for optimal performance despite offering enhanced flexibility. Variational-Bayes (VB) techniques address the identification of outliers in multiple sensors through a probabilistic framework, although with challenges, such as slow convergence and high computational needs, due to iterative optimization per step for VB [16], [17]. The evolution of outlier mitigation, incorporating advanced filtering, such as the enhanced EKF [18] and robust variational Bayesian filter [19], has improved robustness, however, these methods, while effective in multiple dimension outlier rejection, often struggle with the balance between computational efficiency and the risk of discarding valid data. Techniques, such as regression-based exclusion [20] and the cubature Kalman filter [21], also face similar tradeoffs, emphasizing the ongoing challenge in outlier detection and mitigation in both single and multiple dimensions of measurement vector. In this letter, we propose a novel robust filtering method where we use an explicit model for outlier occurrence in measurement dimensions followed by maximum a posteriori (MAP)-based infer-ence. In order to handle all measurement dimensions, the estimator is modified to evaluate only two probabilities per dimension, which reduces the computational cost significantly. Further, for detection of outliers, we derive an equivalence between the normalized innovation ratio (NIR) test [12], [22] and our proposed MAP-based estimator, providing us a very simple rule to detect the presence of outliers. We call our method the NIR-EKF because it uses an NIR-based threshold for outlier detection within the proposed MAP-based EKF. We further evaluate our algorithm against robust filters for accuracy and computational efficiency. Our approach has the following key advantages over prior works. 1) It is fast and has extremely low computational cost. 2) It handles the outliers in multiple dimensions. 3) It is robust to outliers. 4) It is easy to implement and straightforward to apply.
